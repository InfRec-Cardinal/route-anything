# X2_residual_coverage -- eq:residual, thm:marginal, prop:marginals, app:static-adaptive on CodeRouterBench

Script: `run.py` (numpy/pandas/scipy/matplotlib only; master seed 20260911; 500 bootstrap task resamples per context; runtime 9.3 s). Outputs: `results.json` (every number below, with 95% percentile CIs), `figures/*.pdf` (vector, paper style: Times + usetex OK), this README (generated by `run.py`; numbers are not retyped).

## 1. Data and setup

* Real, execution-verified matrix `$ACROUTER_DATA/coderouterbench/id_results_long.csv`: 9999 tasks x 8 models (score in [0,1], token-price `cost_usd` estimates, `latency_ms`). Splits: probing 7080, id_test 2919. Cross-checks asserted in code: Always-Opus on id_test = $34.02 and mean score 43.83 (CodeRouterBench paper). 148 zero-cost Qwen3-Max rows (missing token records) are kept as recorded; nothing is imputed.
* Reference policy B = always `claude-opus-4-6` (Y_B, C_B = its score and dollars). Candidates m in ['Qwen3-Max', 'kimi-k2.5', 'qwen3.5-plus', 'glm-5', 'MiniMax-M2.7', 'gpt-5.4', 'claude-sonnet-4-6']; `cheap4` = ['Qwen3-Max', 'kimi-k2.5', 'qwen3.5-plus', 'glm-5'] for the submodularity lattice.
* Verifier: exact, E_m = {score_m >= theta}, theta in {1.0, 0.5}. Portfolio S: all members are called in parallel; an exact selecting verifier (OR semantics, eq:residual) commits the highest-scoring passing member; on a miss Opus is called and its output committed (eq:accounting with H = 1[any member passes], Y_F = Opus score, C_F = Opus cost). Dollars D = sum of cost_usd of every call made in the epoch (all members + Opus on a miss); primary bill a(S) = E[sum of member costs]; d_m(S) = a(S+m) - a(S) = E[cost_m] (no aggregation fee is charged; the verifier is the benchmark's own grader). Value V = committed score. eta = E[V]/E[D] (ratio of sums).
* Primitives per portfolio (paper notation): p(S) = P(hit); v_h(S) = E[V | hit]; v_m(S) = E[Opus score | miss] (Opus value on S's failure set); b(S) = E[Opus cost | miss]; r_m(S) = P(E_m and all of S fail) (eq:residual). Contexts: 9 dimensions and `overall` (pooled). A candidate is *usable* in a context if it has >= 20 passes and >= 20 failures (code_understanding at both theta and code_refactoring at theta = 1 have no usable candidate: their proxy-metric scores essentially never reach the threshold). An (S, m) case is *non-trivial* if #(E_m and fail S) >= 10, #fail(S+m) >= 10 and #hit(S) >= 10 (otherwise a conditional mean is estimated from < 10 tasks); trivial cases are counted separately.
* Bootstrap: task multinomial resampling within the context; all CIs are 2.5/97.5 percentiles of 500 resamples. Out-of-sample: primitives (p, r_m, v_h, v_m, b, d_m, eta) estimated on probing; realised quantities and their CIs on id_test.

## 2. What is tested, and how each test is labelled

| kind | meaning here |
|---|---|
| IDENTITY | holds by algebra on any sample; reported as a code check only (a1-a3, b3 at theta = 1, e1) |
| IN-SAMPLE hypothesis | does a stated hypothesis of the theorem hold on the data (b1-b3: common v_m, b, v_h across S and S+m; c2: positive error correlation; e2: adaptive marginals) |
| OUT-OF-SAMPLE prediction | primitives on probing, realised outcome on id_test (d1-d3; held-out part of c1/c3) |

### Prediction formula with plugged-in primitives (thm:marginal / eq:app-marginal-identity)

With the *current* portfolio's values (v_h, v_m, b of S), the theorem predicts eta(S+m) - eta(S) = N_S / (E[D(S)] + d_m - r_m b) with N_S = r_m(S)[v_h - v_m + eta(S) b] - eta(S) d_m(S), and sign(eta(S+m) - eta(S)) = sign(N_S). Worked example, `overall`, theta = 1, S = {Qwen3-Max} (eta(S) = 60.07 per $, v_h = 1.000, v_m = 0.361, b = $0.00993):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| kimi | 0.0451 | 0.00082 | +0.0066 [0.0011, 0.0115] | +0.76 [0.12, 1.32] | +0.13 [-0.64, 0.98] | 0.361 -> 0.344 | 0.00993 -> 0.00976 | yes |
| qwen35p | 0.0724 | 0.00170 | -0.0127 [-0.0207, -0.0042] | -1.37 [-2.27, -0.45] | +5.04 [3.45, 6.64] | 0.361 -> 0.354 | 0.00993 -> 0.00853 | NO |
| gpt54 | 0.0506 | 0.00589 | -0.2913 [-0.3097, -0.2749] | -21.27 [-22.75, -19.82] | -22.81 [-24.29, -21.32] | 0.361 -> 0.331 | 0.00993 -> 0.00989 | yes |

The qwen3.5-plus row is a genuine sign disagreement in the pooled context: adding it removes algorithm/test-generation tasks (where Opus is expensive) from the failure set, so b falls by 14% and the realised gain is positive although N_S < 0. The gpt-5.4 row shows the typical case: the hypothesis is violated (v_m falls) but the sign and magnitude are right because d_m dominates.

## 5. Circularity statement

* a1-a3, e1 and b3 at theta = 1 are identities (or hold by construction: a committed hit at theta = 1 has score 1) and carry no evidence about the theory or the data.
* b1-b3 test the theorem's *hypothesis* directly (conditional means on two different failure sets). b4 is not an identity because N_S uses S's (v_h, v_m, b) while the realised change uses S+m's; if b1-b3 held exactly, b4 would be an identity. Its non-trivial content is therefore 'how wrong does the sign rule get when its hypothesis is violated by the measured amount'.
* In (c), Spearman(r_m, p(m0+m)) = 1 is an identity and is reported only to say so; the in-sample 'top-quality != top-coverage' fractions are post-selection (winner's curse) and are confirmed held-out (partners chosen on probing, gaps on id_test).
* All of (d) is out-of-sample: every ranking statistic is computed on probing tasks only (usability included), every realised gain and CI on disjoint id_test tasks.
* No router decision files are used (the question is which capability to add to a fixed verifier-select portfolio, not how a learned router dispatches).

## 6. Limitations

* Prices are the CSV's token-price `cost_usd` estimates (CN price list for the Chinese models; see E8's audit); relative statements involving d_m of Qwen3-Max / kimi / qwen3.5-plus / MiniMax / GLM-5 are specific to that price list. The 148 zero-cost Qwen3-Max rows lower its d_m by ~1.5%.
* The verifier is the benchmark's ground-truth grader, so v_h = 1 at theta = 1 by construction and every hit is value-dominant; a real verifier would add false accepts/rejects. No noisy-verifier variant is run here because none of the tested hypotheses (common v_m, b; residual coverage; submodularity) concerns verifier error.
* Aggregation/verification cost is set to zero (d_m = E[cost_m]); the theorem allows any d_m and the identity a3 would absorb it, but the OOS rankings would shift if a paid aggregator were modelled.
* `overall` pools dimensions with 0% pass rates (code_understanding, code_refactoring at theta = 1), which inflates pooled error correlation and creates the b-drift disagreements; per-dimension numbers are the primary evidence.
* CIs are percentile bootstraps over tasks; in (b) the two compared conditional means share tasks, so the paired CI of the drift is what is reported. Small failure sets (bug_fixing, multi_language at theta = 1) make the v_m and b estimates noisy, which is why the non-trivial filter exists.
* The dimension 'cells' of the record model (Sec. 5) are not used here; contexts are dimensions, not the 21 dimension x source cells.
