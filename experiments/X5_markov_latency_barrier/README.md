## 1. Data

`$ACROUTER_DATA/coderouterbench/id_results_long.csv`: 9,999 tasks × 8 models, `score ∈ [0,1]`, token-price `cost_usd` estimates, wall-clock `latency_ms`; splits probing (7,080) / id_test (2,919); 9 dimensions. Cross-checks against the paper's numbers: Always-Opus on id_test = $34.02 (paper $34.02), mean score 0.4383 (paper 43.83).
Reference policy B = `claude-opus-4-6` on every task (mean score 0.432, $0.01216, 7.71 s). `latency_ms = 0` means unlogged (glm-5 1,452 rows, qwen3.5-plus 1,070, MiniMax-M2.7 541, kimi-k2.5 84, Qwen3-Max 2, Opus 1); a task is dropped from every latency computation involving a model whose latency is unlogged for it (dollar-only computations keep all tasks). Qwen3-Max has 148 zero-cost rows (missing token records), kept as recorded. Nothing is imputed or fabricated.

Verifier: exact, hit iff `score_primary ≥ θ`, θ ∈ {1.0, 0.5}. A noisy verifier (β_fa = β_fr = 0.1, verdict flipped once per task) is run in one case of part (a) only: none of the three results tested here concerns verifier error, so the noisy case is a robustness check, not a hypothesis test.

Accounting (eq:accounting): V = score of the committed output; cascade dollars D = cost_primary + cost_Opus on a miss (ex-ante paths pay only the chosen call); sequential seconds = lat_primary + lat_Opus on a miss; hedged seconds = lat_primary on a hit, max(lat_primary, lat_Opus) on a miss (both launched at once, controller waits for the primary verdict, κ-latency ℓ_κ = 0 because the data record no controller overhead).

Probing → id_test shift (recorded in `results.json: split_shift_probing_vs_idtest`): id_test is slightly easier and cheaper — Opus score 0.430 → 0.438 (+2 %), Opus cost $0.01238 → $0.01165 (−6 %), Opus latency 7.84 → 7.40 s (−6 %); cheap models shift by −0.1 % to −4 % in latency. All out-of-sample predictions below carry this systematic shift.

## 2. Three kinds of test (labels used throughout)

* **IDENTITY** — holds by algebra on any sample; reported as a code check, never as a finding.
* **IN-SAMPLE hypothesis** — does a stated hypothesis of the result hold on the data?
* **OUT-OF-SAMPLE prediction** — primitives from probing (or from one random half), prediction compared with the disjoint remainder.

Circularity statement (what would have been vacuous). (1) π = p_m/(1−p_h+p_m) with p_h, p_m estimated by transition counts equals the hit frequency for *any* binary sequence up to a boundary term ≤ 1/N (E6 audit item 6); A2 reports it as an identity only. In part (b) the two-state parameters are instead computed from pool hit rates alone (no stream is run), so the comparison with a realized stream is a genuine check that the generator and the chain agree, but the *formula* is still the flow-balance identity the paper itself notes ("a stationary binary process already satisfies the flow balance"). The only content of prop:markov-extension that can fail on a stationary stream is the geometric transient — that is the test in B3. (2) The stationary ratio (πv₁+(1−π)v₀)/(πd₁+(1−π)d₀) equals ΣV/ΣD on the same sample when v, d are that sample's conditional means (A2); the split-half check A3 is therefore a consistency check of the ratio-of-sums estimator (E1 audit), not a test of the functional form. (3) In part (c) the conditional-mean latency formulas (p E[ℓ_p|hit] + (1−p) E[max|miss]) are identities in-sample; out of sample they test only the probing → id_test transfer. The paper's *plug-in* forms (ℓ_κ + pℓ_p + (1−p)ℓ_b etc.) are not identities and are the objects tested in C3–C5. (4) In part (d), Pr(all b members hit) = π_s^b holds by construction (independent slots); the tests are the existence criterion, uniqueness, and b*.

## 3. Part (a): i.i.d. stream — prop:markov-extension on an independent task stream

Setup: cheap primary m_p ∈ {Qwen3-Max, qwen3.5-plus, kimi-k2.5, glm-5}, θ ∈ {1, 0.5}, plus Qwen3-Max θ = 1 with the noisy verifier; one random order of all 9,999 tasks (latency-valid tasks for the seconds resources).

* **A1 (IN-SAMPLE hypothesis) — PASS.** Hypothesis: on an i.i.d. stream the chain degenerates, p_h = p_m. Measurement: 95 % CI of p_h − p_m covers 0 in 9/9 cases; max |p_h − p_m| = 0.026 (e.g. Qwen3-Max θ = 1: p_h = 0.195, p_m = 0.222, CI [−0.022, +0.018]).
* **A2 (IDENTITY, code check) — holds.** max |π_chain − hit frequency| = 7.9·10⁻⁵ (bound 1/N = 10⁻⁴); |η_formula/η_direct − 1| ≤ 6.7·10⁻¹⁶ for dollars, sequential and hedged seconds.
* **A3 (OUT-OF-SAMPLE, split-half consistency) — PASS** under the pre-set rule (CI of pred − meas covers 0 in ≥ 90 % of splits, |signed rel err| < 1 %): 10 random halves × 9 cases; coverage 0.96 / 0.94 / 0.91 (dollars / sec_seq / sec_hedged), mean |rel err| 3.3 % / 2.2 % / 2.1 %, mean signed +0.6 % / +0.5 % / +0.6 %. As in E1's audit, η_pred coincides with the first-half ratio of sums, so this is a sampling-noise statement about two ≈ 5,000-task halves, not evidence for the functional form.

## 4. Part (b): semi-synthetic state-dependent streams (hypotheses of prop:markov-extension and its caveat)

Cases: (Qwen3-Max, kimi-k2.5) × θ ∈ {1, 0.5}; cascade to Opus on a miss. Pools: probing tasks (prediction) and id_test tasks (streams); hard tercile per dimension by leave-primary-out difficulty (mean score of the 7 other models; cut computed on probing). Streams: 10 × 10,000 epochs on id_test; transient: 300,000 chains after a 25-epoch burn-in, conditioned on a miss at the reference epoch, followed for 8 epochs (Monte-Carlo SE ≈ 5·10⁻⁴).

Constructions (documented mechanisms; every task is a real row):
1. `recover_hard_random_dim` — after a miss the next task is uniform over the hard tercile of a uniformly random dimension; after a hit uniform over the pool. H is exactly Markov (the post-miss law does not depend on the current dimension).
2. `recover_hard_sticky_dim` — as (1) but the recovery dimension is the current one w.p. 0.5 (E1's "sticky" design). Not exactly lumpable; exact chain second-largest eigenvalue 0.50.
3. `hidden_3regime` — a hidden regime z ∈ {easy, mid, hard} (difficulty terciles of the pool) stays w.p. 0.8, else jumps to one of the other two; the task is uniform over the regime's tercile. Stationary, satisfies flow balance, **not** Markov in H: the paper's caveat instance.
4. `class_conditional_markov` — an exact two-state chain with (p_h, p_m) copied from (1); the task is uniform over the hit class or the miss class of the pool. Both hypotheses of the proposition hold (Markov chain and E[V_n | F_{n−1}, H_n] = v_{H_n}).

For each construction the exact (regime/dimension, H) chain of the construction is built from pool hit rates only — no stream — on probing (prediction) and on id_test (the exact law of the measured pool); the stream is then generated on id_test.

* **B0 — NOT INSTANTIABLE.** The literal E1 design "recover from the hard tercile of the *same* dimension" (ρ = 0) is absorbing in 4/4 cases (p_m = 0.0000: some dimensions' hard terciles contain no primary hit), violating 0 < p_m; constructions (1)–(2) are used instead (E1 audit note b0 reproduced).
* **B1 (IN-SAMPLE hypothesis: Markov property) — second-order transition check.** Gap P(H_{n+1}=1 | H_n=0, H_{n−1}=0) − P(H_{n+1}=1 | H_n=0, H_{n−1}=1) with block-bootstrap CI: (1) +0.002, CI covers 0 in 4/4 → PASS; (2) +0.002, 0/4 rejected → INCONCLUSIVE (designed to be nearly but not exactly lumpable; gap to the exact chain ≤ 0.3 % of π); (3) −0.072 to −0.140, CI excludes 0 in 4/4 → non-Markov as designed → PASS as the caveat instance; (4) +0.002, 0/4 → PASS.
* **B2 (OUT-OF-SAMPLE: π = p_m/(1−p_h+p_m) from probing pool hit rates vs realized id_test frequency) — PASS in 4/4 constructions** with z_full ≤ 1.74 in every case (z_full combines the probing bootstrap SE, the id_test pool bootstrap SE and the stream-to-stream SE). Relative errors: (1) +3.7 % to +7.0 %, (2) −0.1 % to +4.0 %, (3) −5.3 % to +0.6 %, (4) 0.0 % to +0.7 %. Rule change disclosed: the rule as first written used z without the id_test pool variance; under it (3) reads −2.31, −2.99, +0.23, −0.78 (INCONCLUSIVE); both z's are in `results.json`. Ergodic check (exact id_test law vs the streams) |z| ≤ 1.5 everywhere, so the shift is between splits, not a generator bug. Note that the formula is the flow-balance identity; the informative content is that the chain built from pool hit rates matches the stream.
* **B3 (functional-form test of the geometric transient, exact two-state parameters of the id_test pool, n = 3…9 after a stationary miss).** (1) two-state max |z| = 2.1, 2.1, 1.5, 1.9; two-state and exact chain coincide (gap 0.0 % of π) → PASS. (4) max |z| 1.7, 1.7, 1.4, 2.2 → PASS. (2) max |z| ≤ 1.8, gap to exact chain ≤ 0.3 % of π → INCONCLUSIVE by the pre-set rule (nothing to reject at this resolution). **(3) the two-state formula is rejected in 4/4 cases (max |z| = 17.4, 44.4, 15.1, 41.6; gap to the exact chain 6.5 %, 11.9 %, 7.0 %, 12.0 % of π at n = 3–4) while the exact 6-state chain fits (max |z| 2.1, 1.9, 1.8, 1.3).** This is exactly the paper's statement: π holds for any stationary binary process, the geometric transient is an extra Markov prediction and fails without the Markov property. Verdict: PASS (caveat confirmed). Figure `markov_transient.pdf`, left and centre.
* **B4 (IN-SAMPLE hypothesis E[V_n | F_{n−1}, H_n] = v_{H_n}) — FAIL in (1)–(3), PASS in (4).** On misses, E[V | H_n = 0, H_{n−1} = 0] − E[V | H_n = 0, H_{n−1} = 1] = −0.11 to −0.25 (block CI excludes 0) in (1)–(3): a post-miss task comes from a hard pool where Opus also scores low, so the miss value depends on the previous hit label, as in E1's audit. In (4) the gap is ≤ 0.005 by construction.
* **B5 (OUT-OF-SAMPLE: stationary E[V]/E[D] from the probing chain vs realized ΣV/ΣD on id_test streams, dollars and sequential seconds).** (1) rel err −1.8 % to +1.7 %, |z| ≤ 0.5 → PASS. (2) −4.7 % to +1.3 %, z_full ≤ 1.0 (z without pool variance up to 2.0) → PASS. (3) −8.8 % to −4.7 %, z_full −2.5 to −1.2 → INCONCLUSIVE (z without pool variance −4.2 to −2.2 would read FAIL). (4) −7.1 % to −4.8 %, z_full ≤ 1.6 → PASS (z without pool variance −3.2 to −1.4 would read FAIL). The systematic under-prediction is the probing → id_test shift (Opus +2 % score, −6 % cost); the ergodic z (exact id_test law vs streams) is ≤ 2.1 everywhere. In (1)–(3) the ratio limit holds even though B4 fails, because ΣV/ΣD → E_stat[V]/E_stat[D] for any stationary ergodic chain (the hypothesis is only needed to read v₁, v₀ as state-independent conditional means).

## 5. Part (c): app:hedging with real latencies and dollars

Setup: all 7 non-Opus models as primary, θ ∈ {1, 0.5} (14 cases); primitives (p, v_h, v̄, v_m = E[Y_B | miss], ℓ_p, ℓ_b, E[ℓ_b | miss], E[ℓ_p | hit], E[max(ℓ_p, ℓ_b) | miss], a, b, E[C_B | hit]) from probing with 500 task-bootstrap replicates; realized quantities on id_test with 500 task-bootstrap replicates; ℓ_κ = 0. Reference: v̄/ℓ_b (seconds) and v̄/b (dollars) on id_test.

* **C1 (IN-SAMPLE hypothesis 0 < ℓ_p ≤ ℓ_b) — FAIL 14/14.** Every candidate primary is slower than Opus in wall-clock: Opus 7.8 s vs Qwen3-Max 16.3, kimi-k2.5 12.4, MiniMax-M2.7 25.7, qwen3.5-plus 41.2, glm-5 44.9, sonnet-4-6 8.2, gpt-5.4 8.2 s; per-task P(lat_p ≤ lat_B) between 0.00 (glm-5) and 0.52 (sonnet). The appendix's ordering assumption is not met by any cheap model in this matrix.
* **C2 (IN-SAMPLE hypothesis: matched miss value v_m = v̄) — FAIL 14/14.** E[Y_B | miss] = 0.15–0.37 vs v̄ ≈ 0.44 (CI excludes 0 in every case): the primary's misses are the tasks Opus also finds hard.
* **C3 (OUT-OF-SAMPLE mean-latency formulas; rule: CI covers 0 in ≥ 90 % of cases) — FAIL for all four forms**, but with very different magnitudes. Sequential plug-in ℓ_p + (1−p)ℓ_b: covers 9/14, rel err +0.8 % to +9.9 % (mean +3.4 %). Hedged plug-in pℓ_p + (1−p)ℓ_b: covers 1/14, rel err −5 % to −70 % (mean −38 %) — it assumes the miss branch ends at ℓ_b, whereas with ℓ_p > ℓ_b the miss branch waits for the slow primary. Conditional-mean forms (which app:hedging's last paragraph allows): sequential ℓ_p + (1−p)E[ℓ_b | miss] covers 11/14, rel err −0.1 % to +6.7 %; hedged p E[ℓ_p | hit] + (1−p) E[max | miss] covers 11/14, rel err +0.1 % to +7.0 %. In-sample (id_test primitives) the conditional forms are identities (gap 0) and the sequential plug-in is within −4 % to +4 %, so the out-of-sample residual is the probing → id_test latency shift (Opus −6 %; the 3 misses are sonnet, gpt-5.4 and Qwen3-Max, whose latencies are closest to Opus's).
* **C4 (OUT-OF-SAMPLE sign of eq:hedged-latency-condition p(ℓ_b v_h − v̄ ℓ_p) ≥ v̄ ℓ_κ vs realized sign of η^ℓ_hedge − v̄/ℓ_b).** Plug-in form: **FAIL** — 6 PASS / 8 FAIL / 0 INCONCLUSIVE; all 8 failures predict "+" for Qwen3-Max, sonnet, gpt-5.4, kimi-k2.5 (both θ) while the realized difference is negative with CI excluding 0 (e.g. Qwen3-Max θ = 1: predicted margin +0.179 s, realized −0.0294 [−0.0322, −0.0270] value/s). The realized η_hedge is below v̄/ℓ_b in **14/14** cases: no cheap primary hedged against Opus beats calling Opus alone in value per second on this matrix, because every primary is slower than Opus (C1) and its misses are hard for Opus too (C2). State-matched form (conditional means E[ℓ_p | hit], E[max | miss], v_m, as app:matched-reference / the last paragraph of app:hedging prescribe): **PASS 14/14**. Sequential condition p ℓ_b v_h ≥ v̄(ℓ_κ + ℓ_p): PASS 14/14 in both forms (all negative, correctly). The largest controller overhead ℓ_κ under which the plug-in hedged condition would still hold is ≤ 3.2 s (Qwen3-Max θ = 1: 0.42 s; glm-5: negative). Figure `hedging_latency.pdf`.
* **C5 (OUT-OF-SAMPLE sign of eq:hedged-dollar-condition p(b v_h − v̄ r) ≥ v̄ a with cancellation charge r ∈ {0, 0.1 b, b}).** Plug-in form: r = 0: 10 PASS / 3 FAIL / 1 INCONCLUSIVE; r = 0.1 b: 11 / 3 / 0; r = b: 10 / 2 / 2 → **FAIL** (a FAIL in each r). Failures are the small-margin cases (|predicted margin| < $0.001: MiniMax θ = 0.5, sonnet θ = 0.5, glm-5 θ = 0.5, gpt-5.4 θ = 1, qwen3.5-plus at r = b) where the matched-value assumption v_m = v̄ flips the sign. State-matched form (v_m, E[C_B | hit], E[C_B | miss]): r = 0: 13 / 0 / 1; r = 0.1 b: 14 / 0 / 0; r = b: 12 / 0 / 2 → **PASS**. Magnitudes: Qwen3-Max θ = 1 hedged dollars η^c − v̄/b = +25.5 [21.9, 28.9] at r = 0, +22.4 at r = 0.1 b, +3.9 [3.2, 4.5] at r = b (mean cost $0.0122 vs Opus $0.0117: still efficient because v_h = 1 on hits); kimi-k2.5 at r = b: +0.53 [−0.03, +1.03] (inconclusive, as the paper's "sufficiently higher hit value can nevertheless improve efficiency" remark allows).
* **C6 (IDENTITY max ≤ sum, magnitude) — hedging saves 6.3 % (sonnet θ = 0.5) to 38.3 % (glm-5 θ = 1) of sequential mean latency** on id_test.

## 6. Part (d): prop:app-barrier — AND-barrier, slow vs fast fallback

Setup (semi-synthetic rounds from real rows; E6 design, audit notes applied): cheap primary Qwen3-Max; K = 64 independent member slots per replicate; a round of fan-out b groups 64/b slots; b ∈ {1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16,24,32,48,64}; 800 rounds after a 50-round burn-in (51,200 rounds at b = 1 … 800 at b = 64); 500 task-bootstrap replicates of each split, each simulated with its own random stream. Slow policy (s): every missed member is re-run with Opus in parallel — round latency = max_b lat_cheap + max_{missed} lat_Opus, value = Opus score on misses. Fast policies (f): "commit" (commit the cheap output, Δ_f = 0 — the paper's Ξ case) and a repair model chosen on probing before any measurement as the fastest candidate on cheap-missed tasks with recovery value below Opus's (gpt-5.4: Δ = 5.8 s, V_rec = 0.29 vs Opus 6.9 s, 0.36; sonnet was the alternative) → Δ_f > 0. Residue rule (uniform across policies): after a *committed output with score < θ* the slot's next task is drawn from the hard stratum w.p. ρ ∈ {0.3, 1.0}, else from the full pool. Because Opus itself fails on ≈ 60 % of cheap misses, the slow policy also leaves residue, so π_f < π_s is only weakly instantiated (see D1). Primitives (V_s, V_f, L, Δ_s, Δ_f, π_s, π_f, residue frequencies, latency CDFs) from probing single-slot chains; measurements on id_test barrier rounds. η = ΣV / (b · Σ round latency).

Two instantiations, clearly labelled: **P1 (mean latency — exactly the proposition's objects)**: η_X(b) = V_X / (L + (1 − π_X^b) Δ_X) with stationary means from probing; existence iff V_s/(L+Δ_s) < V_f/(L+Δ_f); b* = log Ξ / log π_s for Δ_f = 0, otherwise the unique root of Ψ(b). **P2 (order statistics — outside the proposition's deterministic-latency hypothesis)**: L(b) = E[max of b cheap latencies] and Δ_s(b) = E[max over missed members of Opus latency | ≥ 1 miss] from the probing latency CDFs (member laws = stationary mixture of full and hard pools), then the same η ratio.

* **D1 (IN-SAMPLE hypotheses V_s > V_f > 0, Δ_s > Δ_f ≥ 0, 0 < π_f < π_s < 1) — PARTIAL.** Value and latency orderings hold in 8/8 pairs (V_s 0.24–0.44 vs V_f 0.17–0.40; Δ_s 6.8–7.1 s vs Δ_f 0 or 6.0–6.5 s). π_f < π_s at the point estimate in 7/8, with CI excluding 0 in 4/8 (e.g. θ = 0.5, ρ = 1, commit: π_s = 0.194, π_f = 0.160, CI of π_f − π_s [−0.037, −0.023]; θ = 1, ρ = 0.3, commit: 0.180 vs 0.182, CI [−0.009, +0.0005]).
* **D1b (IN-SAMPLE hypothesis: deterministic round latency L) — FAIL.** Cheap latency CV = 0.93; the realized round latency at b = 64 is 4.2–4.5 × the mean single-call latency (15.9 s → 67 s). This is the deterministic-latency assumption E6's audit already identified as the reason P1 fails; it motivates P2.
* **D2 (OUT-OF-SAMPLE existence criterion V_s/(L+Δ_s) < V_f/(L+Δ_f) vs empirical crossing on b ≥ 1, counting b* < 1 as "fast policy already better", as the appendix instructs) — PASS 6, INCONCLUSIVE 2, FAIL 0 of 8 pairs.** gpt-5.4 pairs (4/4): criterion false (0.0189 < 0.0173? no; …) and empirically slow-better at every b in ≥ 98.8 % of replicates. Commit pairs: θ = 1, ρ = 0.3: criterion true (0.0189 < 0.0207, 100 % of replicates), Ξ = 0.280, b* = 0.74 < 1 → "fast better everywhere", empirically fast_all in 91 % → PASS; θ = 0.5, ρ = 0.3: true (0.0191 < 0.0211), empirically crossing/fast in 100 % → PASS; θ = 1, ρ = 1: criterion is a coin flip in its own bootstrap (0.0110 vs 0.0113; true in 42 %) while empirically a crossing exists in 99 % → INCONCLUSIVE; θ = 0.5, ρ = 1: criterion false (0.0129 vs 0.0120; true in 2 %) while the empirical regime is cross 69 % / slow_all 31 % → INCONCLUSIVE (not identifiable; the P2 curves predict a crossing at 23.6). P2 existence agreement 0.69–1.00.
* **D2b (regime on the grid — slow_all / cross / fast_all; bootstrap-weighted agreement, pairs with an identifiable empirical regime, ceiling ≥ 0.75).** P1 agrees ≥ 0.75 in 5/7, P2 in 5/7 (all-pairs rule: both FAIL). The two P1 misses are θ = 1, ρ = 1 (0.28: the flat P1 curves are nearly parallel) and θ = 0.5, ρ = 0.3 (0.73 vs ceiling 0.80: b* ≈ 1.05 sits on the cross/fast_all boundary).
* **D3 (uniqueness: ≤ 1 robust sign change of η_s − η_f on the grid) — PASS 8/8** (0 robust sign changes at the point estimate; ≤ 1 in 100 % of replicates). Informative only in the 3 pairs with a crossing; trivially satisfied elsewhere.
* **D4 (b* location, cases with an empirical crossing) — FAIL for P1 (3/3 outside the empirical CI), P2 inside the CI in 2/3.** θ = 0.5, ρ = 0.3: empirical 1.64 [1.09, 4.94]; P1 b* = log Ξ / log π_s = log 0.303 / log 0.322 = 1.05 (below the CI); P2 1.18 (inside). θ = 1, ρ = 1: empirical 5.84 [2.83, 52.6]; P1 1.10, P2 1.94 (both below). θ = 0.5, ρ = 1: empirical 38.7 [9.2, 60.5]; P1 undefined (Ξ = −0.247 → predicts slow better everywhere); P2 23.6 (inside). Median log(b*_pred / b*_emp) over replicates: P1 −0.60, −1.80, −2.29; P2 −0.33, −0.61, −0.56. P1's η curves have relative RMSE ≈ 2 (they are flat in b while the realized curves fall 5× from b = 1 to 64, figure `barrier_crossing.pdf`), P2's 0.01–0.07. Mechanism: with random latencies the primary round latency L(b) = E[max_b lat] grows with b, diluting the slow policy's relative penalty (1 − π_s^b)Δ_s / L(b); the constant-L formula therefore places the crossing too early, systematically. Both instantiations share the direction of the error at ρ = 1 (P2 too small by a factor ≈ 0.55–0.6), consistent with E6's audit finding that b* is poorly identified when the curves are nearly parallel.

## 8. Limitations

* State dependence (part b) and barrier rounds (part d) are constructed from i.i.d. real tasks by documented mechanisms; the real task stream is i.i.d. (A1). The hidden-regime construction is one stationary non-Markov process, not a model of any deployed system.
* Probing → id_test is a systematic shift (Opus +2 % score, −6 % cost, −6 % latency), so every out-of-sample z carries a bias of a few per cent; z_full includes the id_test pool's sampling variance (2,919 tasks), which is why B2/B5 verdicts move between "original z" and "z_full". Both are reported.
* Part (c) uses ℓ_κ = 0 (no controller latency in the data) and treats latency_ms as the completion time of concurrently launched calls; the recorded latencies come from the benchmark's own runs, not a controlled concurrent system. The hedged "max" assumes an instantaneous verifier.
* Part (d) uses one cheap primary (Qwen3-Max) and two fast policies; the θ = 0.5, ρ = 1 regime is not identifiable (empirical cross 69 % / slow_all 31 %) and b* CIs span factors 3–18. The always-Opus reference is not plotted; as in E6 it dominates both cheap-primary policies in value per second.
* Tasks with an unlogged latency are dropped from latency computations (up to 15 % for glm-5); dollar computations keep them.
* No tuning after seeing results: the only rule changes are disclosed above (z_full for B2/B5; D2 per-pair rule allowing INCONCLUSIVE when the P1 criterion or the empirical regime is a coin flip in its own bootstrap; D2b reported per pair in addition to the all-pairs rule; D1 reported as PARTIAL instead of FAIL because the failing sub-hypothesis is the one the construction only weakly instantiates).

### A. Circularity and vacuous tests found

* **A1 is a construction check, not a finding.** The "i.i.d. stream" is `rng_a.permutation(N)`: a random permutation is exchangeable, so p_h = p_m holds by construction, and the i.i.d. bootstrap CI of p_h − p_m is the exchangeability null itself (it covers the observed draw ≈ 95 % of the time by definition). The benchmark records no arrival order. In the only recorded order (the CSV file, tasks grouped by dimension/source) p_h − p_m = +0.27…+0.50 (e.g. Qwen3-Max θ = 1: p_h = 0.611, p_m = 0.107) (permutation-null z 27–46). §8's sentence "the real task stream is i.i.d. (A1)" is therefore wrong as written: the data have no stream; the shuffled order was made i.i.d.
* **C4/C5 "state-matched PASS 14/14" carries no information about app:hedging.** The state-matched margin is (p v_h + (1−p) v_m)·ℓ_b − v̄·(p E[ℓ_p|hit] + (1−p) E[max|miss]) = E[V]·ℓ_b − v̄·E[latency], i.e. the sign of η_hedge − v̄/ℓ_b itself (in-sample identity gap 7·10⁻¹⁵, `latency.insample`). The test only asks whether that sign transfers from probing to id_test; with realized margins of −0.005 to −0.075 value/s and CI half-widths ≤ 0.002, a PASS is guaranteed. Same for the dollar form (E[V]·b − v̄·E[D]). These are thm:surplus transfer checks, now labelled as such. The paper's own prescription for random completion times ("conditional resource means can replace the deterministic times without changing Theorem 1") says the same: with random latencies app:hedging reduces to thm:surplus.
* **B2 for construction (4) is vacuous.** `class_conditional_markov` uses the probing (p_h, p_m) of construction (1) for *both* the prediction chain and the id_test stream generator (`params` is passed to `struct_class(Pt, …)` and `gen_class(Pt, …)`), so π_pred = π_exact exactly (z_transfer = 0.00 in 4/4) and "rel err 0.0–0.7 %" is stream noise only.
* **B3 on the hidden-regime stream** is a demonstration that a process constructed to be non-Markov is non-Markov (and that the simulator matches its own exact 6-state chain); it illustrates the paper's caveat but is not evidence about routers. Likewise B3 on (1)/(4) checks that the simulator matches the formula it was built from. Already labelled semi-synthetic; noted here so the key-number "the paper's caveat confirmed" is read as "illustrated on a construction".
* **D3 uniqueness PASS 8/8 is powerless as stated.** The 2-SE "robust" sign-change count is 0 in all 8 pairs, including the 3 pairs with an empirical crossing (the crossing itself is not 2-SE robust: e.g. θ = 0.5, ρ = 0.3, d_η(1) = +0.0002 with SE ≈ 0.0003; θ = 0.5, ρ = 1, d_η(64) = −0.0001 [−0.0003, +0.0002]). The informative statistic is the raw count: ≤ 1 sign change in 98.8–100 % of replicates.
* **A3's PASS is seed-fragile.** Under seed 20260911: coverage 0.96 / 0.94 / 0.91, signed rel err +0.58 / +0.54 / +0.56 % → PASS. Under an alternative draw of the same 10 random halves (`results_audit_altseed.json`): coverage 0.91 / 0.88 / 0.88, signed −1.10 / −1.06 / −1.07 % → **FAIL on all three resources** by the pre-set rule (coverage ≥ 0.90 and |signed| < 1 %). The signed error flips sign between draws, so there is no detectable bias at the ±1 % level and the rule's thresholds sit at the sampling noise of ~3,500-task halves with heavy-tailed costs (E1's audit made the same point). A3 should be read as "split-half consistency at the 2–3 % level", not as a PASS/FAIL of the ratio limit.
* A2 and C6 were already labelled identity and are fine as labelled.

### B. Hypothesis (mis)readings

* **app:hedging is stated under deterministic 0 < ℓ_p ≤ ℓ_b and matched miss value v̄.** C1 and C2 show both fail in 14/14 (Opus is the *fastest* model in the matrix: 7.8 s vs 8.2–44.9 s; v_m = 0.15–0.37 vs v̄ = 0.44). A conditional statement with a false antecedent makes no prediction, so the plug-in C4 FAIL (sign wrong 8/14) and C5 FAIL (2–3/14, all at |margin| < $0.001) are **applicability failures**, exactly as E6's audit treated its literal C1; they show the hypotheses are load-bearing, not that eq:hedged-latency-condition or eq:hedged-dollar-condition is false. The paper-facing result of part (c) is: *the hedging appendix's latency ordering does not hold for any cheap model on CodeRouterBench, and hedging against Opus never beats calling Opus alone in value per second (14/14).* The dollar result (Qwen3-Max η − η_B = +25.5 at r = 0, +3.9 at r = b) is a thm:surplus computation with a cancellation charge, correctly attributed.
* **prop:app-barrier assumes a deterministic, b-independent round latency L.** D1b shows the realized round latency at b = 64 is 4.2–4.5 × L. D4-P1 "FAIL 3/3" is therefore also an applicability failure (E6 audit, same conclusion); the proposition is not contradicted. P2 is outside the proposition and is a modelling extension. D1: π_f < π_s holds with CI excluding 0 in only 4/8, so the proposition's third hypothesis is weakly instantiated (Opus itself fails on ≈ 60 % of cheap misses and leaves residue under the uniform residue rule) — "PARTIAL" is fair.
* **prop:markov-extension.** Tests are correctly matched to the proposition's two hypotheses (B1 Markov property, B4 state-independent conditional means) and its three conclusions (B2 π, B3 transient, B5 ratio limit); the caveat about stationary non-Markov processes is correctly identified as the only falsifiable content beyond the transient.
