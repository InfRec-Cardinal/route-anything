## 1. Setup (all outcomes and costs are real; nothing is simulated except arrival frequencies in Sec. 6)

* Data: `id_results_long.csv`, 9,999 tasks × 8 models, probing 7,080 / id_test 2,919. Cross-checks: always-Opus id_test total $34.02, Opus id_test mean score 0.4383; probing v̄ = 0.4297, c̄ = $0.012375, η_B = 34.73 per $.
* Reference B = claude-opus-4-6 (always). Pool of cheap models = the other 7; F_max = 7. Verifier: exact, score ≥ θ, θ ∈ {1.0, 0.5}; noisy verifier (β_fa = β_fr = 0.1) in Sec. 7.
* Positions k: the 9 dimensions (main) and the 21 dimension × source-benchmark cells. Positions have disjoint tasks, so the objective Σ_k w_k p_k(F_k) is exactly separable (the paper's "independent positions" hypothesis holds by construction).
* Unit at k = one more prepared model in a verified cascade (Alg. 1). Two unit orders, both fixed on probing: **price order** (cheapest-first by mean probing `cost_usd`, the task specification; the order differs across positions, e.g. kimi-k2.5 is cheapest in bug_fixing, Qwen3-Max elsewhere) and **coverage order** (greedy by marginal at-least-one coverage on probing at θ; along it the discrete marginals are non-increasing by submodularity, so the concavity hypothesis holds on probing by construction — the E3 audit variant).
* p_k(F) = P(at least one of the F prepared models passes), F = 0..7, measured on probing; p_k(0) = 0. Weights w_k = ν_k (probing share of k; objective = stream hit rate) in Secs. 2–4; app:budget-scope weights in Sec. 5.
* Prices: unit (γ_k = 1, B = slots 1..63) and dollar (true preparation cost C_k(F) = ν_k Σ_{f≤F} mean cost of the f-th model; the closed forms need a scalar γ_k = C_k(7)/7; 48 budgets up to Σ_k C_k(7)).
* Fits on probing (least squares in p, F = 0..7): shifted power law 1 − p_k(F) = d_k(1+F)^{−r} with common r and free d_k ∈ (0,1] (`common_free`), common r with the literal d_k = 1 − p_k(0) = 1 (`common_literal`), own r_k (`own_free`); exponential 1 − p_k(F) = d_k e^{−ϑ_k F} with own ϑ_k. Also refit on F = 1..7 (E3's convention) for comparison. Search box for r: [1e-3, 30]; a value at a bound means r → 0 or r → ∞. CIs for exponents from 500 probing-task resamples within position.
* Allocators (all from probing quantities only): `dp_probe` exact integer optimum of Σ w_k p̂_k(F_k) by multiple-choice-knapsack DP (dollar costs rounded up to 1 µ$); `closed_common_free` / `closed_common_literal` = eq:power-allocation with the active-set procedure eq:power-active-set, capped at 7, projected to integers within the true budget (floor → largest fractional parts → by g_k while affordable; if non-ascending unit prices make the floored point unaffordable under the true cost, lowest-g units are dropped first); `kkt_power_own` = prop:allocation's KKT condition w_k p_k'(F_k) = Λγ_k solved by bisection on Λ with own r_k and box [0,7]; `kkt_exp_own` = eq:exponential-allocation with own ϑ_k; `greedy_probe` = discrete equal-marginal rule on the empirical curves; `uniform` = equal F_k (dollar mode: budget-exhausting, remainder round-robin); `dp_test` = DP on id_test curves (in-sample ceiling, not a policy); `dp_fit_common` / `dp_fit_own` = integer optimum of the fitted curves (isolates integer/projection loss from misspecification).
* Evaluation: every allocation is scored on id_test as the mean over tasks of 1[cascade of F_k hits]. 95% CIs from 1,000 id_test task resamples, paired across methods, allocations held fixed. "Mean gap" = average over the budget sweep.

## 3. What fails and what survives on this benchmark

Fails: the shifted-power-law family with a common exponent (C2), strict concavity of the price-ordered cascade curve (C1), the closed form eq:power-allocation both against the exact optimum and against uniform (C3, C4), the proportionality rule (C7), and the fixed-hit/miss-value premise behind the app:budget-scope weights at θ = 0.5 (C8, in-sample). The exponential family (eq:exponential-allocation) fits no better and allocates worse (C5).

Survives: the equal-marginal principle of prop:allocation when each position's curve is fitted separately (within 1–3% of the exact optimum and 3–15% above uniform, C5), exactly when the discrete curves are concave (greedy = optimum, C5); the closed forms as algebra (C6); the app:budget-scope conclusion that an efficiency-optimal allocation need not exhaust the budget and that the λρ_k charge, not the hard budget, is what stops spending (C8); the asymptotic exponent 1 − 1/z on a fine Zipf family, together with the paper's own caveat that it is not exact at the allocation range (C9).

## 4. Circularity statement

The hit indicator 1[score ≥ θ], the coverage curves p_k(F), the fits, the unit orders, the weights ν_k, the app:budget-scope primitives (v_h, v_m, b, ρ_k), λ = η_B and the exact J profile used by `dp_J_probe` are all computed on the probing split; every allocation is scored on id_test; `dp_test` is the only in-sample quantity and is labeled a ceiling. C6 is algebra (code check). C7 compares the closed form's structure with the optimum of the empirical probing problem (in-sample structural check). C9's frequencies are constructed; its coverage numbers are occupancy quantities and use real task identities and real Δ_j only.

## 5. Limitations

* F is capped at 7 (7 cheap models); the paper's problem has no upper box. The continuous closed form exceeds the cap at 27–43% (unit) / 69–77% (dollar) of budgets and is projected.
* Dollar-price closed forms use the linearized γ_k = C_k(7)/7 while C_k(F) is nonlinear (and non-ascending under the coverage order, where the integer projection has to repair infeasible floors); DP, greedy and uniform use the true C_k(F).
* θ = 1.0 is unattainable in code_understanding (continuous proxy metric, max 0.614) and nearly so in code_refactoring; p_k ≡ 0 violates "increasing" and W_λ < 0 excludes the position, consistent with the paper's exclusion of nonpositive margins. θ = 0.5 avoids this and reaches the same verdicts.
* Own-r_k estimates at 0.001 or 30 are search-box bounds, not estimates.
* Bootstrap CIs resample id_test tasks with allocations fixed (evaluation noise only); probing-side noise enters only through the exponent CIs.
* The penalized-KKT integer projection (floor, then add a fractional unit only if the fitted marginal exceeds λρ_k) is a heuristic; the exact penalized integer optimum is `dp_J_probe`.
* Within a position the prepared models compete for the same coverage (the paper's own remark that this is not a model of redundant proposers); this is why the price-ordered curves are non-concave.
* Zipf families are semi-synthetic: real tasks, constructed frequencies; the fine family has J = 2,919 cells, so for z = 1.5 the truncation is visible beyond E ≈ 1.6e4.
* Noisy-verifier rows have no CIs. Only 1,000/500 bootstrap resamples.

**Hypothesis misreading 2 (C7).** The sentence after prop:allocation, "with common z and large funded allocations, eq:power-allocation assigns capacity in proportion to (w_k d_k/gamma_k)^{z/(2z-1)}", describes the closed-form solution of the fitted model; it is not a claim about the empirical integer optimum.
