# X11_residual_coverage_routerbench -- identifiability of residual coverage on RouterBench (eq:residual, thm:marginal, prop:marginals, rem:frechet)

Script: `run.py` (numpy/pandas/scipy/matplotlib only; fully offline; master seed 20260917; 500 bootstrap task resamples per context and split, 500 rather than 1,000 because three bootstraps (full, fit, held-out) are drawn for each of 66 contexts; runtime 120.6 s on the M4). Outputs: `results.json` (every number below with 95% percentile CIs), `figures/*.pdf` (+ `.png` previews), `summary_for_paper.md`, this README (generated by `run.py`).

## 1. Setup

* **Data.** RouterBench 0-shot pickle `$ROUTERBENCH_PKL` (36497 prompts x 11 models; score column `<model>`, cost column `<model>|total_cost` in dollars; 86 `eval_name`). Cross-checks asserted in code: gpt-4-1106-preview mean score 0.7814 and mean cost $0.003293 per prompt (RouterBench / CONVENTIONS). No score or cost is NaN or zero; 16 prompts have duplicated text (different `sample_id`) and are kept as recorded.
* **Reference / fallback** B = always `gpt-4-1106-preview` (Y_B, C_B = its score and dollars); it is called and committed whenever the portfolio misses. **Primaries** for the portfolios S (|S| in 1..2): mistralai/mixtral-8x7b-chat, zero-one-ai/Yi-34B-Chat, claude-instant-v1, gpt-3.5-turbo-1106 (the protocol's four) plus the cheap extras WizardLM/WizardLM-13B-V1.2, meta/llama-2-70b-chat; every statistic is also reported for the four-primary subset (`main4`). **Candidates** m: all other non-reference models (10 models incl. claude-v1, claude-v2, code-llama-34b, mistral-7b). 21 portfolios x candidates = 174 (S, m) cases per context.
* **Verifier and cascade** (as X2): exact selecting verifier, E_m = {score_m >= theta} with theta = 0.5 (main) and theta = 1.0. All members of S are called in parallel; the verifier commits the highest-scoring passing member; on a miss gpt-4 is called and committed. D = sum of member costs + (1-H) C_gpt4; a(S) = E[sum member costs]; d_m(S) = E[cost_m] (no aggregation fee); V = committed score; eta = E[V]/E[D] (ratio of sums). Primitives: p(S), r_m(S) = P(E_m & all of S fail), v_h(S) = E[V | hit], v_m(S) = E[Y_gpt4 | miss], b(S) = E[C_gpt4 | miss].
* **Thresholds.** 95% of the contexts have only 0/1 scores, where E_m at theta = 0.5 and theta = 1 are the same events; theta = 1 is therefore run only in the contexts with fractional scores (overall, grade-school-math, mt-bench, rag-other: GSM8K scores in {0, .25, .5, .75, 1}, MT-Bench in tenths, `consensus_summary` and `chinese_idioms` in quarters) and its rows are identical to theta = 0.5 everywhere else.
* **Contexts** (65 + pooled `overall`): the 8 source families below and every MMLU subset with >= 100 prompts (57 of 57; the smallest MMLU subsets have exactly 100). The `overall` context pools everything and is reported separately (X2 showed that pooling heterogeneous costs inflates the b-drift).
* **Split.** Every prompt is assigned once to fit (70%) or held-out (30%) by stratified sampling within strata `eval_name x 1{gpt-4 score >= 0.5}` (seed 20260917): fit 25542, held-out 10955. Contexts inherit the assignment, so each context is itself split ~70/30. OUT-OF-SAMPLE = primitives (p, r_m, v_h, v_m, b, d_m, eta, N_S, rankings, usability, the non-trivial filter) on the fit split, realised quantities and their CIs on the held-out split.
* **Filters** (X2's): a model is *usable* in a sample if it has >= 20 passes and >= 20 failures; an (S, m) case is *non-trivial* if #(E_m & fail S) >= 10, #fail(S+m) >= 10, #hit(S) >= 10. Sign agreement is reported on all non-trivial cases and on *CI-decided* cases (the held-out realised change has a bootstrap CI excluding 0), always next to baselines. Cost basis: dollars throughout.

### 3.3 C3 -- Frechet range of r_m and the excess cost of a marginal-only selector (rem:frechet), theta = 0.5

**Interval (IDENTITY on the same sample).** For S = {m0}, m0 in the six primaries and every usable m: 2348 pairs in 65 contexts, realised r_m inside [max{0, p_m - p_0}, min{p_m, 1 - p_0}] in 100.0% (and in 100.0% of bootstrap replicates). Position (r - l)/(u - l): median 0.32, 10-90% [0.18, 0.48]; within 1% of the lower bound in 0.6%, of the upper bound in 0.0%. The independence proxy sits at median position 0.58; the realised r_m is below it in 93% of pairs (positively correlated errors). Median interval width 0.275 (10-90% [0.154, 0.428]) -- the marginals leave most of r_m unidentified. `fig_frechet_intervals.pdf` shows the 8 families for the four protocol primaries.
**Out-of-sample diagnostic.** The held-out r_m lies inside the interval computed from the *fit-split* marginals in 96.1% of 1991 pairs (its held-out CI overlaps the fit interval in 85.3%); the misses are sampling noise in the small MMLU subsets (families: 98.0% of 402).
**Excess cost of the marginal-only selector (IN-SAMPLE).** For every primary m0 and every pair of usable candidates with |P(E_m1) - P(E_m2)| < 0.02 (1130 triples in 64 contexts), the excess is evaluated in the proposition's accounting (perfect fallback of cost b = b({m0}) = gpt-4's mean cost on m0's failure set, equal candidate costs, unit values): the minimax marginal-only selector chooses m1 with probability q = x/(x+y), x = (u_1 - l_2)^+, y = (u_2 - l_1)^+, and its realised excess over the informed choice is b[max{r_1, r_2} - q r_1 - (1-q) r_2]. Excess <= exact bound b xy/(x+y) in 100.0% and <= the equal-marginal formula (b/2)[min{p, 1-p_0} - max{0, p-p_0}] at p = (p_1+p_2)/2 in 100.0% -- implied by the interval identity, since the bound is the worst case over the intervals. Median excess/bound 0.05 (10-90% [0.01, 0.15]); excess exactly 0 in 8% of triples; median excess $9.63e-06 vs median bound $1.82e-04 per prompt. **Width dependence:** Spearman(excess/b, width) pooled over triples +0.226 [CI +0.151, +0.275]; Spearman(excess $, bound $) +0.469 [CI +0.429, +0.538]; within-context Spearman median 0.09 over 59 contexts with >= 5 triples, positive in 73% (families only: pooled +0.366, within-context median 0.31). The realised excess grows with the width but stays far below the worst case: the observed joint laws are nearly exchangeable (both candidates' successes concentrate on the same easy prompts), so r_1 and r_2 are close even when their Frechet ranges are wide (`fig_excess_vs_width.pdf`).

**Out-of-sample selector test (C5, prop:marginals content).** Same triples defined on the fit split (884 in 60 contexts), realised on the held-out split: mean excess fallback cost of the marginal-only minimax selector $3.06e-05, of the r_m-informed selector (argmax fit-split r_m) $2.13e-05 (ratio 0.70), of a uniform coin $3.06e-05; mean difference marginal - informed $9.34e-06 [CI $5.37e-07, $1.75e-05]; informed better in 492, marginal better in 284, ties 108 (p = 7.7e-14); the fit-split informed pick is the held-out best partner in 62%. Paired outcomes carry real but modest information about which equal-marginal partner to add.

## 4. Hypothesis violations measured (C4)

* **Common v_m (thm:marginal hypothesis).** Over 6392 non-trivial (S, m) cases in 65 contexts (full sample, theta = 0.5), the CI of v_m(S+m) - v_m(S) excludes 0 in 56% (violation frequency); the drift is negative in 90% with median -0.0686 (-11.5% of v_m(S)), 10-90% [-26.4%, -0.1%]. Mechanism (X2 b1): the prompts a new member covers are the ones gpt-4 also tends to solve, so gpt-4's mean score on the *remaining* failures falls. Families only: 79% of 1195; MMLU subsets: 51% of 5197 (smaller samples, wider CIs).
* **Common b.** CI excludes 0 in 18% (median 0.3%); families 49%, `overall` 92% (pooling heterogeneous per-family gpt-4 prices makes b move with the failure set's family mix). **Common v_h.** CI excludes 0 in 5% (max |drift| 0.073; v_h = 1 by construction in binary contexts).
* **Consequence for the sign rule (in-sample b4).** Sign agreement 98.6% on all cases, 99.9% on 5182 realised-CI-decided, 99.9% on 5133 both-CI-decided (baselines on realised-CI-decided: independence proxy 95.1%, always negative 42.1%). Magnitude: median |relative error| of the predicted change 7.4% (within 25% in 80%); substituting S+m's v_m alone reduces it to 2.7%, S+m's b alone to 6.2%. The violated hypothesis biases the magnitude, rarely the sign.
* **Error correlation (X2 c2).** P(E_i^c & E_j^c) - P(E_i^c) P(E_j^c) > 0 in 87% of 1864 usable pairs (CI > 0 in 76%, CI < 0 in 5.5%; median excess 0.068); families only 92% of 360. RouterBench is less uniformly positively correlated than CodeRouterBench (99%): the significantly negative pairs involve the weakest models (mistral-7b, code-llama-34b, WizardLM-13B) against the Claude family or gpt-3.5 on MMLU / HellaSwag, e.g. mmlu: claude-inst x mistral7b (-0.014); mmlu: claude-v1 x mistral7b (-0.010); mmlu: claude-v2 x mistral7b (-0.030); hellaswag: wizard13b x gpt35 (-0.021). This is exactly the regime in which r_m carries information the marginals do not (C3a: median position 0.32 vs independence 0.58).

## 6. Limitations and deviations

* RouterBench's grader is the benchmark's own scorer (exact match / execution / GPT-4 judge for MT-Bench), so the exact selecting verifier is an oracle: r_m(S) here is the *coverage* content of thm:marginal, not the verifier's selection probability. Costs are the benchmark's recorded per-prompt dollars (no latency), so only the dollar basis is reported.
* C3a and the bound part of C3b are identities of the empirical joint law (labelled IDENTITY / IN-SAMPLE); the empirical content is the *position* of r_m inside the range, the width dependence of the excess and the out-of-sample tests C1, C2, C5. The excess in C3b is computed in the proposition's stylised accounting (perfect fallback at b({m0}), equal candidate costs, unit values); the realised dollar difference between pairing with m1 or m2 additionally involves d_1 - d_2 and the drift of b, which C1 covers.
* Pooled Spearman CIs are percentile intervals of the statistic recomputed on task-resampled data; with few triples (theta = 1 rows: 3 contexts) they can exclude the point estimate. Within-context correlations are weak (median 0.09) because equal-marginal triples inside one context share p_0 and hence have similar widths.
* 500 bootstrap resamples (CONVENTIONS allows 500 for heavy computations; three bootstraps per context). The 70/30 split is one draw (seed 20260917); no cross-fitting. MMLU subsets have 100-1,534 prompts, so their held-out splits (30-460 prompts) decide fewer cases by CI and their fit-split intervals miss the held-out r_m more often than the families'.
* theta = 1.0 duplicates theta = 0.5 in every binary-score context and is therefore reported only for the 4 fractional-score contexts; the equal-marginal tolerance 0.02 and X2's usability / non-triviality filters are conventions, not results. The VLM-ExecRouterBench replication of the protocol (P2) was not attempted (data not available locally).
